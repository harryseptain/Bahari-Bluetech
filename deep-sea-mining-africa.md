Global interest in seabed minerals is accelerating at a pace that most African policymakers are not yet prepared for. The cobalt, nickel, manganese, and rare earth elements locked in polymetallic nodules on the ocean floor represent a potential resource windfall — and a governance minefield.

## What Are We Talking About?

The deep seabed beyond national jurisdiction — known as "the Area" — is administered by the International Seabed Authority (ISA), a UN body headquartered in Kingston, Jamaica. Member states can apply for exploration licenses through the ISA, allowing state-sponsored and commercial entities to map and eventually extract mineral resources from the seabed.

## Why Africa Should Pay Attention

Africa's exclusive economic zones (EEZs) extend 200 nautical miles from the coast, giving coastal states sovereign rights over both the water column and the seabed within that zone. Kenya's EEZ alone covers 142,400 km² of the Indian Ocean — an area larger than the entire country of Greece.

The challenge is not the resources. It is the institutions, the regulatory capacity, and the technical expertise needed to govern them responsibly.

## What Needs to Happen

1. **African states must participate actively in ISA rule-making.** The Mining Code is being revised now. Nations that are absent from those negotiations will be governed by frameworks written without their input.

2. **Capacity building in marine geology and seabed mapping.** Kenya, Tanzania, and Mozambique urgently need baseline bathymetric surveys of their EEZs.

3. **Community consultation frameworks** must be established before exploration licenses are granted in proximity to coastal fishing communities.
